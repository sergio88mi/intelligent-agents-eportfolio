# Learning outcomes and where the evidence is

The module has four learning outcomes. This page says, for each one, what I did and where it can be checked.

**1. Identify and critically analyse agent-based systems, differentiating between architectures and approaches.**
My [Discussion 1 posts](forums.html#cd1) compare deliberative, reactive and hybrid architectures against the system I built, and argue that our Retrieval Agent is closer to a structured function than to an agent when measured against Wooldridge and Jennings (1995). The [Discussion 2 posts](forums.html#cd2) compare agent communication languages with method invocation, and the [Unit 6 dialogue](unit06.html) puts KQML and KIF to work on a small example.

**2. Apply and critically evaluate intelligent agent techniques to real-world problems, particularly where technical risk and uncertainty is involved.**
The [research agent](research-agent.html) is an end-to-end system for literature search. Its evidence folder records what went wrong in the live services (a retired model, rate limits, quotas, a source refusing every search) and what was done about it. The [critical evaluation](research-agent.html#evaluation) compares the week 7 design with the week 11 build, including what was not measured.

**3. Deploy critically appropriate software tools and skills for the design and implementation of an agent-based system, bearing in mind applicable legal, social, ethical, and professional issues.**
The system is plain Python with 113 unit tests and sixteen functional tests. The [case studies](case-studies.html) cover explainable decisions, bounded autonomy and bias in the screening step. My [Discussion 3 posts](forums.html#cd3) and the [Unit 10 post](unit10.html) deal with fabrication, responsibility, the EU AI Act and the social effects of a deployed deep learning model.

**4. Systematically develop and implement the skills required to be an effective member of a development team in a virtual professional environment, adopting real-life perspectives on team roles and organisation.**
The [team project](team-project.html) page records how Group D worked, my contribution (the class diagram and the review of the design), what the tutor said, and what I would change. The [reflective piece](reflection.html) is honest about the part of this I did badly, which was timing.

Reference: Wooldridge, M. and Jennings, N.R. (1995) 'Intelligent agents: theory and practice', *The Knowledge Engineering Review*, 10(2), pp. 115–152. Available at: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0269888900008122
